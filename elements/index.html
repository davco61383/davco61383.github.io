The 118 elements: a reading guide

This page is the text companion to the Elementarium periodic table. It lists every one of the 118 known chemical elements with its atomic number, symbol, group, period, chemical family, and atomic weight, followed by a short profile of each element: what it looks like, where it is found or used, its key physical properties, and a couple of facts worth remembering.

The periodic table is arranged by atomic number, the number of protons in the nucleus. Rows are called periods and columns are called groups. Elements in the same group have the same number of outer electrons, which is why they behave alike: the alkali metals in group 1 all react with water, and the noble gases in group 18 almost never bond. The two rows usually drawn below the main table, the lanthanides and actinides, are the f-block, or inner transition series.

The ten chemical families

Elementarium colors each tile by its chemical family. Here is what each color means.

Alkali metals

Group 1, except hydrogen. Soft metals that give away one electron and react hard with water. Members: Lithium, Sodium, Potassium, Rubidium, Cesium, Francium.

Alkaline earth

Group 2 metals. Still reactive, but steadier than the alkali metals next to them. Members: Beryllium, Magnesium, Calcium, Strontium, Barium, Radium.

Transition metals

The wide central block. Often hard, shiny, conductive, and the source of colored compounds. Members: Scandium, Titanium, Vanadium, Chromium, Manganese, Iron, Cobalt, Nickel, Copper, Zinc, Yttrium, Zirconium, Niobium, Molybdenum, Technetium, Ruthenium, Rhodium, Palladium, Silver, Cadmium, Hafnium, Tantalum, Tungsten, Rhenium, Osmium, Iridium, Platinum, Gold, Mercury, Rutherfordium, Dubnium, Seaborgium, Bohrium, Hassium, Meitnerium, Darmstadtium, Roentgenium, Copernicium.

Post-transition

Metals past the transition block. Softer, lower-melting, and more covalent in their bonds. Members: Aluminum, Gallium, Indium, Tin, Thallium, Lead, Bismuth, Nihonium, Flerovium, Moscovium, Livermorium.

Metalloids

The staircase between metals and nonmetals. Semiconductors, including silicon, live here. Members: Boron, Silicon, Germanium, Arsenic, Antimony, Tellurium, Polonium.

Reactive nonmetals

The elements of air, water, and living tissue — plus hydrogen, which sits above the alkali metals but is not one. Members: Hydrogen, Carbon, Nitrogen, Oxygen, Phosphorus, Sulfur, Selenium.

Halogens

Group 17. Sharp, reactive elements that form salts with metals. Members: Fluorine, Chlorine, Bromine, Iodine, Astatine, Tennessine.

Noble gases

Group 18. Full outer shells, so they almost refuse to bond. Members: Helium, Neon, Argon, Krypton, Xenon, Radon, Oganesson.

Lanthanides

The first inner-transition row. Chemically alike, and essential to strong magnets and phosphors. Members: Lanthanum, Cerium, Praseodymium, Neodymium, Promethium, Samarium, Europium, Gadolinium, Terbium, Dysprosium, Holmium, Erbium, Thulium, Ytterbium, Lutetium.

Actinides

The second inner-transition row. All radioactive, from thorium and uranium through the synthetic elements. Members: Actinium, Thorium, Protactinium, Uranium, Neptunium, Plutonium, Americium, Curium, Berkelium, Californium, Einsteinium, Fermium, Mendelevium, Nobelium, Lawrencium.
